Provenance

Author

Fernando Lessa Magalhães

Updated

September 14, 2026

Introduction

Provenance concerns where material came from, who contributed it, in what role, how it was transformed, and what evidential status it carries. The engineer and the agent formed several provenance rules while preserving a source corpus, attributing interpretations, and separating factual records from reconstruction and analysis. Those rules have different scopes and must not be collapsed into one general authorship rule. The central boundary, accepted by the engineer, is that what belongs to the engineer remains the engineer’s and what belongs to the agent remains the agent’s.

Scope

Provenance concerns the origin, speaker, role, transformation, order, and evidential status of material. It preserves differences among source text, agent interpretation, marked reconstruction, derived analysis, and later storage representation.

Provenance does not by itself prove truth, authorship beyond the stated scope, current filesystem state, or engineer adoption of a derived formulation.

Development through interaction

1. A source-pool authorship default became the first local boundary

The engineer described a long-lived branch as a pool containing the engineer’s prompts and other materials. For that historical pool, the engineer proposed treating Markdown and text as the engineer’s material by default, while PDFs, PDF-derived material, or material named for another person were external bibliographical references. The agent confirmed the mapping with explicit exceptions, and the engineer called it their first boundary (Segments 0178–0184).

This rule is explicitly local to that historical source pool. It must not be generalized to every Markdown file or later corpus.

2. Preservation preceded movement and mapping

The engineer changed the request from a map to a local copy of the full corpus. The agent later proposed keeping the source stable before tree movement, and the engineer directed that the archive remain the first priority (Segments 0190–0207).

The agent later reported completing that archive. Those reports are not independently verified here.

3. Intended meaning was separated from frequency

When the engineer emphasized that the larger corpus should preserve the engineer’s intended direction, the agent proposed consolidating by provenance and role rather than raw occurrence count, with the engineer’s messages as primary evidence for intended meaning (Segments 0378–0386).

That weighting rule is an agent proposal. The engineer asked what raw frequency meant but did not explicitly adopt the full method in that exchange.

4. Interpretation received an explicit attribution rule

The agent attributed an inference to the engineer. The engineer corrected it, welcomed interpretation as concept-forming work, and challenged why the agent should not label interpretation as a human would. The agent stated a symmetric rule, and the engineer accepted it as the core idea: what is the engineer’s remains the engineer’s, and what is the agent’s remains the agent’s (Segments 0504–0521).

This is the principal contribution-attribution boundary formed in the interaction.

5. Factual record, reconstruction, and analysis were separated

The engineer required a complete factual record of the exchange because compilation would lose material. When the engineer proposed filling speech gaps, the agent required the factual record to remain untouched and any gap-filling to be marked as reconstruction. The engineer explicitly praised and accepted that strategy. Derived academic analysis was placed beside, not inside, the source (Segments 0705–0718).

6. The live session displaced existing docs as the source of meaning

When the agent treated existing tree material as authority, the engineer corrected that it was only an example and said that the live session being created carried the relevant provenance. The agent then stated that session meaning should generate the classification bottom-up (Segments 0922–0947).

7. Source-bound repair exposed a coverage limit

The engineer instructed that every represented domain be checked against the source that created its meaning, so repair would not drift over earlier drift (Segment 1019).

The agent then reported that the available record did not cover earlier material and began explaining that the lineage frame had come from the conversation (Segments 1023–1026). That coverage mismatch was later resolved for the recorded interval. Evidence outside that interval and the authorship of the derived lineage remain unresolved.

Current formulation

Established through the interaction:

  • source and transformation roles must remain visible;
  • the historical-pool authorship default is scoped and permits exceptions;
  • interpretation must be marked and attributed;
  • the factual record must remain separate from reconstruction and analysis;
  • session-derived classification must follow the live session rather than existing docs;
  • every domain repair must return to the source that created its meaning.

Agent formulations and limits:

  • provenance supports attribution and backtrace but does not alone establish truth or adoption;
  • a later complete capture can resolve an earlier coverage warning without erasing that earlier report;
  • lineage should preserve composite contributions until authorship is explicitly resolved.

Relations and interfaces

Related domain Relation Boundary
Authority, Capability, and Delegation Attribution identifies who contributed or decided. Source attribution does not transfer authority.
Conceptual Exploration Interpretations may contribute new concepts. They remain agent formulations unless adopted.
Drift Backtrace returns to earlier evidence when meaning becomes unstable. Provenance does not prescribe the correction.
Concept Lineage Method Lineage records transformations over time. The frame’s authorship remains unresolved.
Content Derived session pages are Content artifacts. They are not source records or solely engineer-authored text.

Open questions

  • Who creates and owns the derived lineage: the engineer, the agent, or a provenance-preserving composite process?
  • What evidence exists outside the recorded interval?
  • How should later engineer adoption of an agent formulation be recorded without erasing its origin?
  • What evidence can support a readable reconstruction when speech is incomplete?
  • When does provenance weighting become an analytic method requiring separate validation?

Limits

The factual record is complete only for the recorded interval, and speech recognition remains noisy. Repository, archive, and shared-session states are agent reports unless separately verified, and the historical authorship default cannot be extended beyond its named pool.
