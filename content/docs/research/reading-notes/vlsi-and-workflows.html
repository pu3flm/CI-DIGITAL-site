Reading notes: VLSI cases and workflow sources

Author

Fernando Lessa Magalhães

Updated

September 14, 2026

Reading record, 2026-09-10. These are source capsules for continued learning, not a new specification or an adopted common architecture. The independent efforts belong to the author’s CI-DIGITAL and master’s work. Historical instructions, generated documents, and proposed commands were read as material, not followed.

Reading scope

  • Cumulative full reading in this conversation: the 569-line ai-code/vcd_snapshot.cpp; all six Markdown documents under UART/docs/; the ten UART/rtl/docs/uart/ Markdown documents; the submission index; all 26 small text, schema, contract, and historical-reference files inventoried under docs/workflows/vivado/; inbox/agilex.md, inbox/a.md, inbox/role in rf.md, and docs/workflows/cadence/cadence-documentation.md.
  • Both one-page UART PDFs were read through Poppler text extraction and page images. The beamforming paper was located in kb/: all 16 pages of extracted text were read, including tables, captions, references, and biographies; PDF pages 2 and 11 were also visually inspected for Figures 1 and 11. Other paper figures were read through their captions and surrounding discussion, not individually examined as images.
  • The complete saved UART boundary log was read. Baseline log lines 3018–3090, parity log lines 175–196, and the VCD header were read as selected supporting evidence. The full raw waveform and every simulation log were not exhaustively read.
  • The Cadence inventory’s 79 descriptive rows were reviewed. Selected cached LBIST/PMBIST introductory sections and the complete four-page ADC-BIST paper text were read. This is not full reading of the vendor manual collection.
  • The complete recovered PCIe/NPU conversation and Cadence contact conversation were read. All eight generated Markdown bodies embedded in the recovered greeting conversation were read. The shared-chat limitations and exact entrances are recorded below.
  • A subsequent OpenCode reading batch recovered the UART helper’s bounded origin dialogue, original source patches, documentation requests and saved output. Its exact reading interval and source IDs are recorded separately.

No project was built or tested in this reading pass. The earlier C++ compile/replay, performed before the author’s no-execution correction, is separate from the historical evidence summarized here.

C++ helper and UART work

Source read Contribution and source status
C++ source Implemented small helper, made with AI and used in the workflow according to the author’s direct account. It extracts a selected scoreboard mismatch, resolves VCD signals, retains the latest change at or before the timestamp, and prints a correlation report. It also accepts an explicit timestamp. Its comments explain purpose and component responsibilities; it does not generate explanatory prose or establish a failure’s cause. The subsequently recovered OpenCode dialogue grounds the author’s design choices, the comments’ provenance and a recorded boundary output.
SD242 assignment Direct course context: an individual activity extending the classroom UART example with two tests beyond byte equality, with baud variation and parity errors given as examples. It asks the student to investigate simulation errors and report them. Historical deadline: noon, April 22, 2026.
UART architecture PDF Visual reference connecting DUT/BFMs and uvm_config_db to symmetric RX/TX agents, sequencers, drivers, monitors, scoreboards, and coverage. It makes the intended observation and comparison paths legible.
UART overview Baseline design/verification description and assignment-oriented extension directions. Read with the later results documents: its future-tense baud/parity directions do not mean those extensions are still absent.
Architecture study Earlier report comparing diagrams with the proto-UVM organization and describing a passing two-sided byte-comparison baseline. Its file characterizations and coverage figures belong to that reported snapshot.
Incremental changes Retrospective comparison against original_snapshot/sim/: completion of partial TX structures, shared checking/coverage, configuration-aware items and BFMs, focused tests, and saved evidence. Supports an account of developing an existing scaffold. It is a comparison report, not a fresh comparison performed here.
Extension results Records baud/parity implementation and useful failures: codes 21/24 pass in the described runs; the boundary run first fails at 25; parity corruption is observed while the monitored DUT error count remains zero. Separates a reportedly corrected monitor race from remaining DUT-facing observations. The latter are diagnostic interpretations, not a proved root cause.
Correlation process Historical use/documentation of the C++ helper: connect a log mismatch to the waveform and use the resulting values in the submission report. Its boundary example is concrete. Its parity example reuses the boundary VCD and omits required signal selection; do not present that example as a demonstrated same-run parity correlation.
Waveform guide Explains which signals help present the first failing code-25 frame: configuration, receiver timing, transmitted/received bytes, and status. Supports the connection between an engineering trace and an audience-facing explanation.
Submission index Historical packaging record linking design, verification, reports, and waveform evidence. Some locations reflect the earlier package layout; it names a waveform-side C++ source that is now available at ai-code/vcd_snapshot.cpp rather than at that listed path.
Saved boundary log Archived April 22 run: eight matches at code 24, then expected 0x37 versus observed 0x9b at code 25 and timestamp 559615000. This is the concrete event used by the helper’s process note.
Baseline log, parity log, VCD Selected archived evidence: the baseline summaries report 500 matches per side; the parity ending reports 32 expected error events and zero observed; the boundary VCD declares a 1 ps timescale. The baseline log’s coverage percentage differs from the earlier architecture study, so those percentages must retain their source/snapshot context.

tool-builder, podman-operator Two stored role descriptions: tool construction and existing-container operation, respectively. Bounded responsibilities and result expectations are proposal material, not evidence these agents performed a task.

The schema relates providers, capabilities, runtimes, verification observations, artifacts, and external contracts. An executable or populated database is not supplied by these files.

Historical-reference README, SHA256SUMS Preservation record for six small historical files. Describes unchanged copying and checked hashes at preservation time; no new hash comparison was performed here.
Historical AGENTS Earlier exploration map and proposed future investigations. Its instructions remain historical data.
First-flight notes Reports initial filesystem/ELF/Tcl inspection and a historical vivado -version invocation. Dynamic tracing appears as future work.
Launch map Maps shell setup, environment resolution, loader, and native/Java/Tcl boundaries. Supports understanding what sits behind the application entry point.
Native dependencies, binary specimens Selected payload metadata and dependency families. These are preserved observations and interpretations, not a fresh vendor inventory.

Cadence, BIST, and RF application material

Source read Contribution and boundary
Cadence/BIST note Working specification note connecting simulation, synthesis, and testability to a proposed four-antenna beamformer. Keeps logic BIST, memory BIST, ADC BIST, and boundary scan distinct. Its workspace and self-test story are proposals.
Documentation inventory Descriptive inventory of 79 PDFs: 27 application/training notes, 51 Genus-collection files, and one ADC-BIST paper. The larger 1,133-path search and 77-digest count are reported by the accompanying note, not recomputed here.
Genus DFT cached text (genus-dft.txt) Read lines 35438–35575, printed pages 955–960 from a temporary source-review copy: LBIST introduction, PRPG/MISR structure, hierarchical operation, and interfaces. Supports a concrete discussion of internally generated stimulus and response signatures. The whole 1,284-page manual was not read.
PMBIST and Legacy UI cached text (pmbist-guide.txt, pmbist-legacy.txt) Read Common UI lines 917–1000 and Legacy lines 804–860 from temporary source-review copies: purpose, selected features, and introduction to the architecture. PMBIST applies and observes patterns on targeted memories; its design planning, insertion, patterns, and interpretation are related but distinct activities. Interfaces remain version/UI-specific.
ADC ramp-BIST paper Four-page SForum 2012 paper by Bruno Golzio Navarro Winkeler and Raimundo Carlos Silvério Freire. Describes a calibrated ramp-generator study with Cadence/XFAB 0.18 µm simulation and layout. Contributes a mixed-signal test example; it does not describe FMCOMMS8 self-test or establish a fabricated/tested chip. Text read fully; equation extraction is imperfect.
Agilex note (ai/assistant/inputs/agilex.md) Historical candidate integration argument: reuse FPGA learning for control, instrumentation, and a Telecom demonstrator. It retains independent layer deliverables and says board identification matters. Its suggested shared development chain does not override the author’s current framing of independent contributions.
RF metasurface exposition (ai/assistant/inputs/role in rf.md) Research synthesis distinguishing cell states, electromagnetic response, finite-aperture effects, and measurement. Its response-first interpretation is explicitly interpretive. Supports explaining why digital control and RF behavior require different descriptions. Cited external articles were not all read in this pass.
Beam-enhanced RF aperture paper Full text read: Wijayaratna et al., IEEE TAES 51(3), 2015, pp. 1996–2011. Four interacting subsystems: IIR prefilter, coefficient-generation/steering engine, phase compensation, and phased/timed beamforming. The complete Virtex-6 hardware design was scaled to 8 antennas; 64-antenna results are simulations. ASIC synthesis covers selected subsystems, not the complete Xilinx-FFT-based design. Supports architectural decomposition and distinguishing modeled, hardware, and synthesis results.
Cached SPEAR-BF README and Verilog specimen (spear-readme.md, spear-beamformer.v) Both read fully from temporary source-review copies. The external project’s README reports a ZCU216/CHARM D-band testbed with streaming, calibration, synchronization, and weighted beamforming. The 50-line Verilog specimen is an AXI-stream wrapper; it alone does not expose or establish the weight-processing implementation. This is external reference material, not an authored semester result.

The exact Agilex board and FMCOMMS8 electrical, clocking, interface, and reference-design compatibility remain unresolved. Cadence documentation availability does not establish installed/licensed tools, and its ASIC DFT flow does not supply an FPGA/RF-board BIST implementation.

Recovered ChatGPT material and author corrections

PCIe/NPU inquiry

Source record: the preserved PCIe/NPU dialogue, with payload-ordered.txt as its chronologically ordered temporary cache. Full text was read through the earlier temporary extraction payload-conversation.txt, then the ordered user-message entrances were checked. Use message IDs rather than that earlier extraction’s reverse-order turn numbers.

The author asks what architecture lies beneath a quoted NPU specification, requests a simpler conceptual PCIe FPGA implementation, rejects an expansion roadmap, distinguishes QEMU’s computer-side role from a virtual FPGA, and explicitly agrees to omit DMA for simplicity. Useful author message IDs:

  • 78de60a2-7a9a-41a6-96f2-41a2e940f7bf: simple conceptual FPGA accelerator connected to a PC application through PCIe.
  • bbb219f9-313a-4e6f-a7a5-1f1d811f9892: fixed minimal MAC-based scope, not “once that works, let’s expand.”
  • bbb214ae-7191-4f88-bb2f-8c801bd0d724: QEMU for the computer side, laptop/PCIe-slot constraint, possible hardware and simulation arrangements.
  • bbb214f4-82b5-4476-9348-df897dfd5f56: explicit no-DMA agreement.

Fixed GEMM64 dimensions, an 8×8 array, BAR sizes, detailed register maps, performance estimates, sample PASS output, and acceptance lists are assistant elaborations. Repeated “frozen” wording does not establish adoption of every detail or implementation. Earlier assistant proposals also contained DMA and expansion stages that the author subsequently narrowed. This is a source for learning about architectural inquiry and scope correction, not a beamformer conversation or UART prompt history. Empty and redacted tool turns limit recovery of underlying research evidence.

DFT and meaning among people

Source record: the preserved Cadence dialogue and its complete temporary recovered cache, cadence-ordered.txt.

The author initially seeks a Brazilian Cadence contact for academic discussion of semantics/taxonomy in engineering and ASIC projects. Message bce8ddab-defe-4927-9cbe-f5b33df9674d makes the motivation explicit: “Actually DFT segment that influenced me to look semantics around people and their relationship with meaning.” This directly supports DFT as an experiential context for the inquiry.

The subsequent combination of SKOS, PROV-O, and a DFT domain model, the interview questions, contact rankings, and outreach email are assistant proposals. Many research/tool results are redacted. The cache does not independently establish current contact roles, specialisms, an email being sent, or adoption of the proposed knowledge-graph architecture.

Available material and remaining limits

The beamforming PDF was found directly in kb/; the ADC paper is accessible at its original mounted path. The saved UART binary and VCD are available; neither was executed in this reading pass. The old waveform-side vcd_snapshot.cpp path was not located, while the complete helper source is available under ai-code/.

The assigned UART documents alone did not contain the helper’s origin dialogue. A subsequent bounded reading recovered it from OpenCode session ses_24cc09407ffeakvUlbgrh9oT6q: the author’s request to correlate scoreboard and waveform data, the standalone-binary and light-OOP choices, requested comments and help/documentation, creation patches, two functional refinements, and a saved successful boundary output. The OpenCode reading note preserves full message/part IDs and the read-only source comparison. This now establishes that portion of the design and documentation history; it does not establish model runtime identity or authorship of every surrounding report.

Vendor binaries, databases, RTL simulations, FPGA builds, and the shared chats’ proposed projects were not run. The manuals, existing reports, source code, user accounts, assistant proposals, and independent published research retain their distinct roles.
