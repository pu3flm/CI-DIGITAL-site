Evidence-First Methodology for a Governed CI-DIGITAL Concept Index and Typed Concept Graph

Author

Fernando Lessa Magalhães

Updated

September 14, 2026

Executive determination

The project should build a governed, longitudinal concept index and typed concept graph, not begin with an ontology and not infer one directly from a language model. The system should preserve raw English-language evidence, compute reproducible lexical and temporal observations, and only then allow reviewed semantic assertions. Its central engineering property is traceability: every published term, concept, relation, mapping, score, and status must be recoverable to a versioned source, a transformation record, a review decision, and an intended use.

The method therefore separates six things that are often collapsed:

  1. Corpus evidence: what a person, document, tool, or standard actually contributed.
  2. Deterministic observations: counts, locations, time intervals, and co-occurrences produced by a pinned procedure.
  3. Semantic annotations: proposed meanings, concept boundaries, domains, and relation types.
  4. Verification results: evidence that an artifact or transformation meets specified requirements against a baseline.
  5. Validation results: evidence that the resulting index or graph serves its intended VLSI/IC design, verification, and test uses.
  6. Governance decisions: human approval, publication, deprecation, and retirement.

This separation resolves the principal defects found in the earlier “Create Concept Index” pilot: a sparse 18-record corpus was insufficient for recurring 2–4-gram conclusions; declared concept and domain assignments were mixed with measured co-occurrences; projected domain edges reused concept-edge evidence; formula changes raised scores without adding evidence; declared membership was represented as certainty; edge-derived graphs lost isolated nodes; historical labels could not be authenticated without original exports; and distinct dates were mistaken for distinct sessions.1

The recommended outcome is deliberately modest in formal commitment. A concept or relation is eligible for an immutable release candidate only when its scoped definition or predicate, direction, evidence, counterevidence, review disposition, and intended use satisfy the applicable profile. An independent approval decision may then authorize a distinct publication event that associates the approved claim version with a release. The artifact qualifies as an ontology only if the project later defines formal classes, predicates, constraints, cardinalities, identity/equivalence rules, and inference semantics and then tests their consequences.

1. Research question, purpose, scope, and users

Research question

How can the project build and govern an evidence-backed index of English lexemes, phrases, concepts, VLSI/IC domains, typed relations, sources, and temporal evolution while preventing provenance contamination, semantic overclaiming, false equivalence, and unsupported semantic claims derived from statistical observations?

Purpose

The method has four purposes:

  • make recurring language and conceptual structures in CI-DIGITAL inspectable;
  • improve retrieval across VLSI/IC design, verification, and test artifacts;
  • create a controlled path from corpus observations to reviewed technical concepts and relations; and
  • provide assurance records sufficient to explain what was accepted, why, under which version, and for which use.

It is not a method for maximizing graph density. A smaller graph with explicit evidence and stable semantics is preferable to a highly connected graph whose edges cannot be distinguished as observations, annotations, mappings, or logical assertions.

Scope

The primary domain is VLSI/IC design, verification, and test, including relevant artifacts, activities, methods, tools, languages, representations, metrics, lifecycle stages, and evidence. Examples include RTL, netlists, layout, timing, power intent, testbenches, assertions, coverage, formal properties, fault models, scan/test access, verification plans, test results, waivers, nonconformances, and design revisions.

The corpus is English-only at the inclusion boundary. Mixed-language records are segmented before admission; an English segment may be included while an adjacent Portuguese segment remains excluded but addressable in the source registry. Original text is never rewritten or deleted by language selection.

Exclusions

The following are outside the domain corpus unless a future, explicit scope decision introduces a separate scheme:

  • telecommunications as a research field;
  • Brazil as a geographic or policy research scope;
  • construction, BIM, and built-environment vocabularies;
  • generic software-engineering vocabularies that are not tied to a stated VLSI/IC use;
  • assistant-generated prose treated as evidence of the corpus owner’s vocabulary;
  • graph topology treated as evidence of causality, hierarchy, or truth; and
  • a renderer, publication framework, or site generator. Markdown and future LaTeX/Hugo/Quarto export are supported by neutral identifiers and links, but no renderer is selected here.

Intended users

  • VLSI/EDA subject-matter reviewers, who decide whether meanings and relations fit authoritative electrical-engineering sources and project use.
  • Verification and test engineers, who use the index to retrieve requirements, plans, testbench elements, coverage evidence, defects, waivers, and results.
  • Corpus curators, who acquire, segment, normalize, and version sources without changing their evidential identity.
  • Data/tool engineers in the interface layer, who implement storage, queries, exports, constraint checks, and reproducible measurements without redefining VLSI concepts.
  • Assurance reviewers and approvers, who examine claims, counterevidence, procedures, configurations, and release decisions.

3. Governing conceptual distinctions

3.1 Lexeme, term, concept, domain, and graph

ISO 704 treats terminology work through relationships among objects, concepts, definitions, and designations, while ISO 1087 supplies the foundational vocabulary for terminology work.45 This methodology operationalizes that distinction as follows:

  • A lexeme is a language unit observed in a source.
  • A term occurrence is a lexeme or phrase at a specific source location.
  • A term record groups variant occurrences without erasing them.
  • A concept is a reviewed unit of knowledge with a scoped definition and designations.
  • A domain is a governed subject-field or concept-scheme context. It is not inferred solely from clusters.
  • A typed concept-graph architecture maintains identified nodes and typed references across distinct named graphs: immutable analytical observations in observation_graph and reviewed semantic claims in reviewed_semantic_graph.
  • A formal ontology adds declared formal semantics that support defined inferences and consistency questions.

RDF provides a graph data model of subject-predicate-object triples, but using RDF does not make a graph an ontology.6 OWL provides ontology constructs with formally defined meaning, yet an OWL reasoner establishes consequences of asserted axioms rather than whether those axioms correctly describe VLSI practice.7 SKOS is neither a step before RDF nor a step before OWL: it is an RDF vocabulary whose data model is defined as an OWL Full ontology.8

3.2 Frequency, importance, and significance

Three dimensions remain separate:

Dimension Evidence basis Permitted conclusion
Lexical recurrence Token/phrase occurrences under a pinned normalization and segmentation configuration “This expression recurs in these sources.”
Conceptual importance Stated retrieval/use requirement, domain authority, risk, or lifecycle role “This distinction is necessary for this governed use.”
User-specific significance User-authored recurrence plus explicit review or selection “This concept is significant to the corpus owner under this criterion.”

The withdrawn ANSI/NISO Z39.19-2005 (R2010) edition separates literary, user, and organizational warrant and ties controlled-vocabulary quality to retrieval purpose, candidate-term review, testing, and maintenance.9 This methodology uses these provisions as historical guidance, not as a current standard, and adapts them as domain-source warrant, corpus-owner warrant, and project-use warrant. A term can be frequent without being important; important without being frequent; or user-significant without being suitable as the preferred VLSI designation.

3.3 Observation, assertion, and truth

An observed co-occurrence states only that two identified items occurred within a declared window. It does not establish:

  • semantic relatedness;
  • broader/narrower structure;
  • part-whole structure;
  • dependence or enablement;
  • causation;
  • validity of either concept; or
  • truth of the surrounding text.

The observation is still useful: it can nominate a separate candidate record and supply corpus evidence for human review. Review, when successful, produces a separate semantic claim linked to the candidate and qualifying evidence. The observation never changes type or status, and later decisions do not retroactively change what was measured.

4. Conceptual architecture

The architecture is a controlled derivation chain with explicit feedback and release gates:

This is an architecture description of selected concerns, not the architecture itself. ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010:2022 expressly distinguishes an entity’s architecture from an architecture description and does not prescribe a development method, notation, tool, or recording format.10 This methodology uses that distinction to maintain multiple views without pretending that a graph picture describes acquisition, governance, storage, review, query behavior, and release control all at once.

Four named graph/views are mandatory and must not be collapsed into an unlabeled default graph:

  1. provenance_evidence_view: source/artifact versions, acquisition activities, derivation runs, versioned procedures/tools/configurations/environments, segments, evidence items, lineage groups, quotations, mirrors, revisions, and agents.
  2. observation_graph: occurrences, measures, co-occurrences, similarity signals, and projected analytical edges produced by named procedures.
  3. reviewed_semantic_graph: accepted concept definitions, typed semantic assertions, and scoped mappings, each backed by an admissible claim package.
  4. release_governance_view: intended uses, reviews, verification and validation records, approval decisions, publication events, releases, changes, deprecations, and retirements.

The same stable identifier may be referenced across views, but records never change from one view’s record family into another. A new derivation, candidate, claim, decision, or event receives its own identifier and typed link to its inputs. In particular, displaying an observational edge beside a semantic edge does not make their epistemic status equivalent.

Architectural components

  1. Source vault: immutable source payloads or authenticated references, checksums, rights/access notes, and acquisition records.
  2. Execution-provenance registry: acquisition activities; versioned procedures, tools, configurations, and execution environments; generic derivation runs with normalized input/tool/output links; and typed specializations such as extraction/parser/OCR runs with page/span maps and quality uncertainty.
  3. Corpus registry: source identity, scope decision, provenance class, language decision, temporal metadata, and relationships among source, segment, quotation, and derivative.
  4. Observation store: tokens, lemmas, n-grams, spans, frequencies, windows, and co-occurrences with procedure/configuration identifiers.
  5. Semantic workspace: candidate terms, proposed concepts, definitions, domains, typed relations, mappings, counterexamples, and review records.
  6. Published registry: stable identifiers, approved representations, versioned concept schemes, release notes, deprecations, and compatibility mappings.
  7. Assurance ledger: requirements, claims, evidence, checks, results, dispositions, approval, and evidence locations.
  8. Interface/export layer: TSV/JSON/SQLite/Parquet now; RDF/SKOS/OWL/SHACL only when the corresponding semantic commitment and checks are justified.

5. Independent domain corpus and interface-layer policy

5.1 Namespace and authority separation

The index must maintain at least three independent namespaces:

  • cid: for governed VLSI/IC design, verification, and test concepts;
  • ext: for concepts retained in independently versioned external schemes; and
  • ifc: for representation, provenance, storage, query, formal-semantics, and tooling concepts used by the interface layer.

cid:verification_coverage may be represented using RDF and described with PROV, but rdf:Property, prov:Entity, database “transaction,” or Computer Science “verification” do not thereby become VLSI parent concepts. A relation across namespaces is an explicit mapping with a scope and purpose. It is never a merged glossary entry.

5.2 Source admission policy

A source may be registered because it is operationally relevant to CI-DIGITAL, but every segment and every proposed cid: concept requires its own explicit VLSI-applicability record. Container-level relevance is only a routing hint. A generic database, software-testing, machine-learning, security, telecommunications, geographic-policy, or project-management passage does not enter cid: merely because it appears inside a CI-DIGITAL document.

A segment is eligible for the VLSI domain corpus only when its exact locator and at least one applicable VLSI facet are recorded and one of these bases is documented:

  • the segment itself defines or constrains a VLSI/IC design, verification, test, manufacturing-test, physical-design, or signoff concept within the cited authority’s scope;
  • a versioned CI-DIGITAL requirement explicitly assigns the segment a VLSI purpose, artifact, activity, method, concern, or evidence role;
  • a VLSI subject-matter reviewer identifies the specific VLSI applicability and the qualifying evidence location; or
  • an external technical source’s exact segment is demonstrably used in a stated VLSI workflow, with the cross-domain conditions recorded.

Authorship by a VLSI body and location inside a VLSI project are not sufficient on their own; only the applicable clause or segment is admitted. If explicit segment-level applicability is absent, the material remains pending, enters ifc: when it describes the interface/tooling implementation, enters an independently versioned ext: scheme when retention is justified, or is excluded. A cid: concept likewise cannot inherit domain membership solely from the source or section that nominated it.

Computer Science methods such as graph algorithms, NLP, databases, formal logic, or provenance may support the system through ifc:. Their terminology remains defined by their own sources. Where a concept is genuinely used in both domains, each definition is retained and a scoped mapping records the correspondence conditions.

5.3 Domain authorities

The IEC’s IEV Part 521 is a primary terminology source for semiconductor devices and integrated circuits.11 IEEE 1800-2023 is the active SystemVerilog design, specification, and verification language standard; IEEE 1800.2-2020 is the active UVM language reference; and IEEE 1500-2022 and IEEE 1838-2019 address embedded-core and 3D-IC test access respectively.12131415 These sources govern only the concepts in their scopes. They do not provide a universal hierarchy for all CI-DIGITAL terms.

Accellera’s UCIS 1.0 is especially instructive: it standardizes coverage-data interoperability and explicitly distinguishes temporal, spatial, and heterogeneous merging; semantically different heterogeneous coverage items should not be automatically merged.16 The index applies the same discipline to concept evidence: storage compatibility is not semantic equivalence.

SEMI’s Compilation of Terms is a candidate locator where manufacturing equipment, materials, process control, traceability, or test interfaces are relevant; a term supplies evidence only when its exact accessible definition/source record and version are captured, and SEMI categories are never imported wholesale into design-verification concepts.17 Si2/OpenAccess and LEF/DEF can anchor design-data representations, while the new Si2 AI-for-EDA ontology can be evaluated as an external, versioned candidate scheme; none is the CI-DIGITAL ontology absent a separate scope decision and admissible claim package.1819

6.2 Evidence lineage and independence

Each evidence item may participate in one or more versioned evidence_lineage_group records because different claims can expose different common origins. A named independence_rule defines which lineage relations are collapsed for one claim or analysis. Independence is therefore claim-relative: two items count as independent support only when they were separately originated and neither derives the supported proposition from the other item or from a common underlying item under that rule.

The following rules are binding:

  • byte-identical mirrors, repository copies, cached copies, and republications belong to the same lineage group;
  • a quotation, excerpt, translation, generated summary, normalized text, extraction, measurement, or graph projection remains in the lineage of its source, even though it receives a distinct evidence-item identifier;
  • document revisions and new editions receive distinct source_version_id values but remain in the same lineage group unless a recorded independence analysis establishes a separately originated evidential basis; a changed passage may be new evidence without becoming an independent source;
  • documents that quote the same standard clause, test report, dataset, benchmark, experiment, or underlying project requirement declare shares_underlying_evidence_with or a more specific dependency and do not multiply independent support for that proposition; and
  • organizational or author diversity is informative but does not establish independence when the items share the same underlying evidence.

Every counted support set therefore exposes its evidence-item membership, independence_rule_id, candidate lineage groups, collapsed equivalence classes, and unresolved dependencies. Unresolved independence is reported as unknown and is never optimistically counted as independent.

6.3 Provenance separation rules

  • User and assistant text are separate through every table, measure, and export.
  • Quoted material is attributed to the quoted source when known and never counted as the quoting actor’s lexical evidence.
  • Uploaded or repository files contribute to file-author/source statistics, not dialogue statistics.
  • Code identifiers and comments are separate channels; they are not automatically natural-language terms.
  • Tool outputs are evidence of a tool run, not user assertions and not external-source originals unless authenticated.
  • Generated summaries are derivatives; they cannot replace the source payload for verification.
  • System/developer messages are governance/runtime context and are excluded from user-vocabulary statistics.

6.4 Time and session uncertainty

The temporal model must store the source value and its interpretation separately:

Field Meaning
timestamp_raw Exact value received from the source.
timestamp_normalized ISO 8601 value only when conversion is justified.
time_zone Source zone, explicit offset, inferred zone, or unknown.
time_precision Instant, minute, day, month, year, unknown.
time_basis Source-declared, filesystem metadata, export metadata, inferred, manual.
time_uncertainty_start/end Interval for uncertain dates.

interaction_group_id Locally grouped records under a documented grouping rule.

Distinct dates can support distinct_date_count; they do not establish distinct sessions. First-seen and last-seen results inherit the least precise relevant timestamp and must expose uncertainty rather than print a false instant.

6.5 Acquisition and derivation execution identity

Acquisition and derivation are linked but distinct activities. An acquisition_activity obtains or authenticates source material and is referenced by every source_version. A derivation_run transforms one or more registered versioned inputs into one or more registered versioned outputs. Neither is represented by an unvalidated string or an identifier whose target registry is absent.

The execution-provenance spine uses independent immutable registries:

  • procedures identify logical methods, while procedure_versions identify the exact executable or manual specification used;
  • tools identify software, services, instruments, or parsers, while tool_versions identify exact releases/builds and integrity locators;
  • configurations identify configuration families, while configuration_versions hold exact parameter sets and hashes;
  • execution_environments identify environment families, while environment_versions capture exact runtime, operating-system/container, dependency, locale, and relevant hardware state;
  • acquisition_activities identify source-obtaining/authentication acts; and
  • derivation_runs identify transformations and link normalized input, tool, and output association records.

Every derivation run identifies input artifact version IDs + procedure_version_id + tool_version_id(s) + configuration_version_id + environment_version_id + actor/operator + start/end times + log/evidence IDs + output artifact identity/hash, with nullability only where the governing procedure explicitly declares a field not applicable. derivation_run_inputs, derivation_run_tools, and derivation_run_outputs preserve roles and multiplicity. A generic artifact_versions registry gives every source or derived version a valid common FK target; each concrete derived table also carries derivation_run_id, constrained to the run that registered its output.

This record supports reproducibility and change impact. It does not prove semantic correctness. A deterministic procedure can reproducibly generate an incorrect result if its rule or input classification is wrong. Dangling procedure, tool, configuration, environment, acquisition, input, output, or derivation references are release-blocking integrity failures.

6.6 Source bytes to extracted text

PDFs, scans, office documents, HTML, and other non-plain-text sources require an explicit derivation before their text can be segmented. extraction_runs is a one-to-one typed specialization of derivation_runs, not a disconnected execution model. The generic run resolves the input artifact_version_id/source-byte hash, procedure version, parser/OCR tool_version_id values, configuration version, environment version, actor, time, logs, and output artifact identity/hash. The extraction specialization adds mode (embedded_text, parser, OCR, or hybrid), source-page or object to output-span mapping, warnings, extraction-quality measures, and uncertainty or unreadable regions. Page headers, footers, columns, tables, formulas, images, and reading order are not assumed correct merely because text was produced.

Extracted text is a derivative evidence item in the same lineage as its source bytes. Corrections produce a new extraction version linked to the prior result; they do not overwrite source bytes or silently repair quotations. Claims cite the exact extraction version and reversible page/span locator, while high-impact semantic evidence requires inspection of the source rendering when extraction uncertainty could alter meaning.

7. Deterministic extraction and statistical measurement

ISO/IEC/IEEE 15939:2017 organizes measurement around explicit information needs, defined measures, analysis, use, and evaluation of whether results are valid.21 This methodology therefore prohibits “interesting metrics” without a decision they support.

7.1 Required sequence

  1. Resolve acquisition procedure/tool/configuration/environment versions, record the acquisition activity, and register/hash the source artifact version or record why hashing is unavailable.
  2. For non-plain-text sources, run a generic derivation with an extraction/parser/OCR specialization and preserve versioned inputs, tools/configuration/environment, page/span map, output identity/hash, quality, and uncertainty.
  3. Segment by message, quotation, code block, document structure, and candidate language boundaries while preserving reversible locators.
  4. Apply deterministic language decisions and segment-level VLSI applicability/admission rules; source- or container-level scope remains routing metadata only.
  5. Preserve raw/extracted text versions and reversible span locators.
  6. Apply a versioned, reversible normalization view.
  7. Tokenize and sentence-segment with a pinned implementation and ruleset.
  8. Produce token, lemma, and contiguous n-gram occurrences.
  9. Aggregate by provenance class, source, authenticated session, interaction group, and time window.
  10. Generate co-occurrence observations under named window definitions.
  11. Generate candidates; do not assign semantic relation types automatically.

Every output-producing step creates a derivation_run and registered output artifact_version; a combined procedure may emit several outputs, but each output retains the same valid producing-run FK and its own identity/hash. A derived table cannot use a free-form run label in place of that FK.

Language detection software may nominate segments for review, but corpus inclusion is deterministic with respect to the stored decision record. A probabilistic language score is never silently converted into a permanent English/non-English fact.

7.2 Reversible normalization

Normalization produces a derived view and never overwrites raw_text. Each normalized token retains its source span and the sequence of applied rules. Rules may include Unicode normalization, whitespace normalization, case-folding for a comparison view, punctuation handling, and a domain-specific protected-token list for forms such as SystemVerilog, UVM, RTL, 3D-IC, IEEE 1800.2, signal names, and HDL operators.

Lemmatization and stemming are analyses, not corrections. Both should retain the surface form. Acronym expansion is an annotation relation (abbreviation_of or expanded_as_in_source), not a normalization that destroys the acronym.

persistence_span Interval between supported first and last observation bounds. Not meaningful as activity continuity; long spans may contain no intermediate use.
window_frequency Occurrences or documents per declared calendar or release window. Fixed window definition and complete-enough acquisition scope.
cooccurrence_count(a,b,w) Number of distinct windows w containing both items. Exact window type, source set, and item spans. It is an observational edge only.
provenance_balance(t) Vector of counts by origin/content role. Detects assistant, quotation, file, or tool dominance; not a quality score.
support_set(c) Unique evidence IDs linked to claim c. Identity-preserving set; projected and duplicate evidence remain marked.

No composite number is called “confidence.” If a future pilot needs ranking, it may compute a versioned support index from declared inputs, but the record must state that the index is ordinal or heuristic, not a probability. Its formula, calibration set, sensitivity analysis, and limitations are configuration items. A formula-only change cannot alter support_set, ISF, or claim/release eligibility.

7.4 Corpus adequacy and temporal claims

Counts are always reportable, but recurrence and trend claims require adequacy gates. For the future pilot:

  • a phrase is “observed” after one occurrence and “recurrent” only under a declared minimum count and source-dispersion rule;
  • no trend direction is issued with fewer than three complete comparison windows;
  • a change is reported with raw window counts and acquisition coverage, not only a percentage;
  • sparse or censored windows are labeled insufficient rather than interpolated; and
  • a source added late to the archive is distinguished from language first used late in the underlying work.

Thresholds are project acceptance parameters, not universal statistical facts. They must be justified against corpus size, retrieval use, false-positive cost, and reviewer capacity.

7.5 Candidate relation generation

Candidate generation may use co-occurrence, lexical similarity, shared source distributions, repeated syntactic patterns, temporal alignment, and model suggestions. Each candidate stores every contributing signal independently. A model may propose depends_on, measures, or implements; the system records that proposal, model/configuration, prompt/input IDs, and rationale in an immutable candidate record. An accepted review may support creation of a separate semantic claim with a new identifier; it never changes the candidate edge into a semantic edge.

A projected concept-to-domain edge is always a derived analytical observation in observation_graph. Projection may summarize accepted or observed lower-level assignments, but it may never masquerade as a semantic domain assertion, enter reviewed_semantic_graph, or increase independent support. If a semantic concept-to-domain relation is needed, it is created as a separate claim with its own identifier, predicate, segment-level VLSI applicability, admissible evidence, counterevidence review, and disposition.

8.3 SKOS policy

SKOS supplies concepts, concept schemes, lexical labels, notes, semantic relations, and cross-scheme mapping properties. Its mapping links are semantic links between concepts in different schemes, and SKOS itself warns that mapping properties do not substitute for provenance management.22

The index may use:

  • skos:Concept for a published concept;
  • skos:ConceptScheme for a governed vocabulary or domain scheme;
  • skos:inScheme for scheme membership;
  • skos:prefLabel, skos:altLabel, and notes after lexical review; and
  • SKOS semantic or mapping properties only after their meanings fit the reviewed assertion.

It must not:

  • convert co_occurs_with into skos:related automatically;
  • convert in_domain_scheme into skos:broader;
  • use skos:exactMatch because labels are identical;
  • infer that every close mapping is transitive; or
  • use a SKOS integrity/entailment result as proof of source-semantic correctness.

8.4 Mapping versus harmonization

ISO 25964-2 addresses mappings among independently maintained vocabularies, while ISO 860 addresses harmonization of concepts, concept systems, definitions, and terms.2324 Mapping preserves source autonomy; harmonization changes or coordinates the systems toward common treatment. This methodology defaults to mapping.

Every “almost the same” record retains:

  • source and target concept IDs;
  • exact source definitions and versions or authenticated locators;
  • source and target schemes and authorities;
  • mapping type;
  • intended use and prohibited uses;
  • correspondence conditions;
  • known representational loss;
  • supporting and counterevidence;
  • reviewer and review date; and
  • validity interval and supersession status.

For example, a vendor’s “coverage closure” and a project’s “verification closure” may support query expansion while remaining unsuitable for evidence aggregation. The mapping is then close_match_for_retrieval, not equivalence.

8.5 Threshold for ontology status

A release may be called an ontology only when all of the following are explicit and versioned:

  1. formal node classes and identity rules;
  2. formal predicate meanings, direction, domain, and range;
  3. class/property axioms and declared inference regime;
  4. cardinality or closed-shape constraints where required;
  5. equivalence and difference rules;
  6. treatment of missing information and the open-world assumption;
  7. consistency and entailment questions the ontology is designed to answer;
  8. test cases for intended and unintended inferences; and
  9. governance for axiom change, re-verification, and re-validation when an intended use could be affected.

OWL’s open-world semantics means absent information is not generally false, and OWL is not a syntax-conformance language requiring fields to be present.25 SHACL can validate an RDF data graph against a shapes graph and produce detailed results, but that establishes conformance to those shapes, not correctness of the VLSI interpretation.26

10.5 Assurance argument

ISO/IEC/IEEE 15026-1:2025 defines the shared vocabulary and concepts for assurance, while Part 2 specifies assurance-case structure and meaning.3031 The index does not need a maximal assurance case for every edit, but a release should have an inspectable argument:

  • Top claim: Release R is fit for approved uses U under limitations L.
  • Subclaim A — evidence integrity: sources are authenticated to the stated level, provenance classes are separated, and derivations are reproducible.
  • Subclaim B — semantic integrity: concepts and relations are supported by scoped VLSI authorities and reviewed counterexamples.
  • Subclaim C — representation integrity: records satisfy the release schema/shape and expected logical consequences.
  • Subclaim D — retrieval utility: representative CI-DIGITAL tasks meet agreed performance and usability criteria.
  • Subclaim E — change control: configuration items, baselines, impact analysis, and required re-verification and/or re-validation obligations are complete.

Each subclaim links to evidence and recorded limitations. A failed or missing subclaim is visible; it cannot be hidden behind verified=true.

12. Configuration, versioning, change control, and lifecycle

ISO 10007 identifies configuration-management planning, identification, change control, status accounting, and audit; it applies from concept to disposal.34 ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288:2023 covers the full lifecycle through retirement and permits iterative, concurrent, and recursive application of processes.35 Applied here, the lifecycle is:

12.1 Acquisition

  • Register source authority, identifier, version, access conditions, payload hash, and acquisition activity.
  • Preserve original payload or state why only a reference/derived record is available.
  • Record completeness boundaries; an export subset is not “all sessions.”
  • Classify source, actor, content role, language, and domain scope without altering raw content.

12.2 Normalization and measurement

  • Baseline normalization rules, tokenizer, protected forms, stop lists, language decisions, grouping rules, and co-occurrence windows.
  • Treat every rerun as a derivation with output identity.
  • Retain prior outputs when a rule changes so formula/rule effects can be compared separately from evidence changes.

12.3 Annotation and review

  • Nominate candidates with evidence and counterexamples.
  • Review definitions before preferred labels when labels could be homographs.
  • Record rejected candidates and mappings so failed proposals do not recur without new evidence.
  • Distinguish review completion from approval for publication.

12.4 Approval, publication, and mapping

  • Create an immutable release-candidate baseline containing all nodes, including isolated nodes.
  • Freeze active definitions, labels, relations, mappings, profiles, constraints, intended uses, query sets, and test results.
  • Record an independent approval decision against that exact baseline and its verification/validation package.
  • Publish machine-readable and human-readable views only through a publication event that references an accepted decision and the same release hashes.
  • Record mapping validity by source/target version and intended use.
  • Preserve the prior successful release until the new publication event succeeds; record failed and rolled-back attempts without rewriting release history.

12.5 Change control, re-verification, and re-validation

A change request states the affected configuration item, rationale, evidence, expected impact, migration treatment, required reviews, and rollback or rejection conditions. Impact analysis traverses dependencies from source and rule through observations, claims, mappings, tests, exports, and releases.

Re-verification checks the changed item against its specified requirement or baseline. It is mandatory when source content/authentication, transformation rules, schemas, inference regimes, concept definitions, predicates, mappings, implementation behavior, or other verifiable configuration properties change.

Re-validation checks fitness for an intended use in its stated users and operational context. It is mandatory, as applicable, when an intended use, user group, operational context, query set, relevance judgment set, retrieval behavior, release limitation, or service interaction changes.

Both impact analyses are required when:

  • an external standard is revised, withdrawn, or superseded;
  • a concept definition, preferred label, semantic relation, mapping, or qualifying evidence changes in a way that may affect retrieval or use;
  • new counterevidence challenges a published assertion or a release-fitness claim; or
  • a release or migration changes both specified behavior and operational use.

The change-impact record selects reverify, revalidate, both, or neither-with-rationale for every affected claim, intended use, and release. A prior validation record never silently transfers to a changed intended use, and a new verification pass never substitutes for that validation.

12.6 Deprecation and retirement

Deprecation preserves identity, prior definition, validity interval, replacement link, rationale, and retrieval behavior. Retirement occurs only after impact analysis addresses indexed artifacts, mappings, saved queries, dependent claims, intended uses, and reproducibility of historical releases; live dependencies block retirement. Deleted records are tombstoned in the registry, identifiers are never recycled, and a failed release or rollback cannot restore an identifier for a different object.

AP-05 Seed a quoted VLSI paragraph in a user message. Terms count toward quoted-source/file views, not user-authored vocabulary.
AP-06 Seed two records on one date with null session IDs. distinct_date_count=1; authenticated session frequency remains unavailable.
AP-07 Seed a source, mirror, quotation, summary, and derived measure sharing one underlying passage. Distinct evidence items remain traceable, but claim-relative ISF=1 under the named rule.
AP-08 Audit every cid-eligible segment and concept. 100% resolve to a versioned segment-level VLSI-applicability record; container-level purpose alone never passes.
AP-09 Traverse every source version and derived artifact through the normalized provenance chain. Each source resolves its acquisition activity; each derived row resolves one producing derivation run; every run resolves input artifact versions, procedure version, all tool versions, configuration version, environment version, actor/time, logs/evidence, and output identity/hash.
AP-10 Seed records with unresolved acquisition, procedure-version, tool-version, configuration-version, environment-version, input/output-artifact, and derivation-run IDs. Foreign-key or import validation rejects every dangling reference; no affected artifact can enter analysis, export, approval, or release.

14.4 Structural and logical tests

  • All identifiers are unique, immutable, and resolve in the release.
  • Required fields satisfy relational/JSON constraints; RDF exports satisfy the versioned SHACL profile when one is adopted.
  • Every published concept has at least one definition or scope note, a language-tagged preferred label for the release language, scheme membership, provenance, a satisfied claim-type evidence profile, review, an independent approval decision, and a successful publication event.
  • Every semantic edge uses a registered predicate with direction, scope, domain/range policy, evidence, review, and status.
  • Every mapping links identified concept versions from different schemes and includes intended use.
  • Expected-inference tests pass; prohibited-inference tests do not appear under the declared entailment regime.
  • Structural conformance, reasoner outcome, semantic review, retrieval evaluation, approval decision, and publication event remain separate records.

14.5 Retrieval and intended-use validation

Create a versioned query set representing at least these CI-DIGITAL tasks:

  • find all sources supporting a SystemVerilog/UVM verification concept;
  • distinguish functional coverage, code coverage, assertion coverage, and test coverage;
  • retrieve verification artifacts by lifecycle role: requirement, plan, procedure, configuration, result, waiver, defect;
  • locate DFT/test-access concepts without conflating verification methodology;
  • trace a published relation to exact evidence and counterevidence;
  • retrieve historical terms after deprecation without assigning them to new content; and
  • show a concept’s observed evolution without using assistant-originated language as user evidence.

Two VLSI/EDA reviewers should independently judge relevance for a stratified sample; disagreements are adjudicated and retained. The pilot reports per-query precision and recall, macro and micro summaries, unanswerable queries, provenance errors, semantic false positives, and reviewer disagreement. Initial release thresholds should be set only after a baseline run exposes task difficulty. The non-negotiable gate is zero known provenance-class or domain-boundary violations in the seeded negative tests.

15. Minimum viable implementation stages and stopping gates

No corpus pipeline is implemented in this study. The recommended staged implementation is:

Stage 0 — Governance and identifiers

Deliver source/classification policies, segment-level VLSI-applicability rules, identifier rules, evidence/claim statuses and admissibility profiles, intended-use records, predicate registry, executor/reviewer/approver/publisher role separation, and configuration-item inventory covering procedures, tools, configurations, environments, acquisition activities, and derivation runs.

Stop gate: do not ingest at scale until user/assistant/quotation/file/code/tool provenance classes and the cid:/ext:/ifc: boundary are approved.

Stage 1 — Immutable corpus registry

Implement source/version/segment and artifact-version registries; acquisition activities; versioned procedure, tool, configuration, and execution-environment registries; generic derivation runs with normalized input/tool/output links; extraction specializations and page/span maps; domain-applicability/admission decisions; evidence lineage; payload hashes; language/scope decisions; and temporal uncertainty.

Stop gate: do not compute user vocabulary until AP-01 through AP-10 and DT-08 pass.

Stage 2 — Deterministic lexical oracle

Implement reversible normalization, protected VLSI forms, token/lemma/n-gram occurrences, TF/DF/ISF/SF/IGF, actor-specific measures, temporal windows, and generic derivation-run manifests for every produced artifact.

Stop gate: do not issue recurrence/trend claims until deterministic tests pass and corpus-adequacy rules are declared.

Stage 3 — Observation graph and candidates

Implement observation_graph with all-node materialization, co-occurrence and distribution observations, projected analytical edges, candidate-term records, and candidate-relation packages. Preserve raw signal vectors and evidence lineage; do not assign a confidence probability.

Stop gate: do not export observational edges as semantic predicates.

Stage 4 — Reviewed concept index

Add definitions, labels, domain/facet assignments, external authority locators, claim-type evidence-profile enforcement, counterexamples, review/adjudication, and immutable nomination/claim/review links.

Stop gate: no concept claim enters a release candidate without accepted scope, qualifying evidence, review, and intended use. Approval remains a separate decision and does not publish it; publication requires the later successful event in Stage 6.

Stage 5 — Typed semantic graph and mappings

Introduce a separate reviewed_semantic_graph, predicate registry, semantic edges, external scheme/version registry, mapping conditions, impact analysis, and source-to-claim traceability.

Stop gate: no mapping or projected relation may manufacture independent evidence; negative semantic tests must pass.

Stage 6 — Representation profiles and service validation

Add stable exports, optional RDF/SKOS, SHACL profiles, retrieval evaluation, release assurance arguments, release_governance_view, independent approvals, publication events, rollback, and lifecycle automation. OWL is introduced only for approved competency questions requiring formal inference.

Publication gate: no event succeeds unless the candidate hashes match the approved baseline, required verification and validation records remain current, the accepted decision authorizes that exact candidate, and a rollback target/procedure is recorded.

Stop gate: do not call the artifact an ontology until the ontology threshold in Section 8.5 and logical test suite are satisfied.

18.1 Domain structure

The index should begin with multiple facets rather than one forced tree:

Facet Illustrative values
Lifecycle/activity requirements, architecture, RTL design, synthesis, physical implementation, verification planning, simulation, formal verification, DFT insertion, ATPG, manufacturing test, post-silicon test, debug.
Physical-design/signoff activity floorplanning, power planning, placement, clock-tree synthesis, routing, parasitic extraction, static timing analysis, signal-integrity analysis, power-integrity/EM-IR analysis, DRC, LVS, ERC, timing/power/physical signoff.
Artifact specification, HDL module, assertion, testbench, stimulus model, coverage model, netlist, floorplan, timing/power constraint, routed layout, parasitic extraction, timing/power/DRC/LVS result, fault model, test pattern, result database, waiver, defect report.
Verification method inspection, lint/static analysis, simulation, assertion-based verification, formal proof/model checking, equivalence checking, emulation, FPGA prototyping, coverage analysis.
Test method scan, boundary scan, embedded-core test, IJTAG/instrument access, memory BIST, logic BIST, ATPG, wafer/final test, 3D stack access.

Evidence role requirement, assumption, procedure, configuration, expected result, observed result, coverage evidence, proof artifact, anomaly, disposition.

Facet membership permits a coverage_model to participate in functional verification, a UVM testbench, and a lifecycle stage without claiming three competing parents. Hierarchies may later be added inside a facet when an authoritative generic, instance, or part-whole relation is established.

18.2 Domain predicate examples

Candidate domain predicates should be narrower than generic related_to:

  • specifies_requirement_for
  • implements_design_intent_of
  • verified_against
  • validated_for_use_in
  • exercises
  • observes
  • measures_coverage_of
  • generates_stimulus_for
  • detects_faults_represented_by
  • provides_test_access_to
  • configured_by
  • produces_result
  • waives_with_rationale
  • supersedes

Each needs a definition and counterexample. For instance, a coverage metric may measure exercised model items without proving requirement satisfaction; a test may detect faults represented by a model without proving absence of all physical defects; and a passing simulation may provide evidence for a requirement without validating silicon in its intended environment.

Predicates such as verified_against and validated_for_use_in are conceptual relations only. Asserting either one never creates a verification or validation record, never supplies a procedure or observed result, and never assigns a pass/fail/fit disposition. Those process records must be independently instantiated under Section 11 and linked as evidence where admissible.

18.3 Example evidence chain

IEEE 1800-2023 / project verification plan / RTL source version
        -> authenticated source segments
        -> observed terms: assertion, cover property, functional coverage
        -> candidate concept: assertion-based verification
        -> reviewed definition and scoped labels
        -> semantic assertions linking artifact, method, and evidence roles
        -> retrieval and traceability tests
        -> approved concept-graph release

The standard anchors language meaning; the project plan anchors intended use; source files and results anchor project evidence. None substitutes for the others.

18.4 Handling domain boundaries in practice

  • Formal methods enters cid: only through a VLSI use such as property checking, equivalence, or SAT-based verification; broader AI/formal-method classifications remain ifc: or ext:.
  • Graph visualization remains a presentation concern. A layout cluster must not become a domain or semantic relation because it looks coherent.
  • Software artifacts inside verification environments can be represented as artifacts without importing a generic software taxonomy as the parent of IC verification.
  • Telecommunications material remains outside cid: unless an exact segment is separately evidenced as applicable to an IC artifact, method, or requirement; shared words such as “network,” “channel,” or “verification” do not establish that applicability.
  • Brazil or other geographic/policy context is metadata or an independent external scheme when needed. Archive co-location and project sponsorship do not turn geography, industrial policy, or regulation into VLSI concepts.
  • AI/ML for EDA claims retain model, dataset, toolchain, design/node, uncertainty, and evaluation context. The repository’s NSF workshop report emphasizes data availability, generalization, scalability, reliability, and robust infrastructure, which should become evidence obligations rather than vocabulary alone.36
  • Industry ontologies such as Si2’s 2026 AI-for-EDA release are mapped as external schemes. Their agentic-workflow purpose and release version remain explicit; only reviewed VLSI meanings are admitted to cid:.37

18.5 Initial CI-DIGITAL release objective

The first useful release is not “all semiconductor knowledge.” It is a small, auditable index capable of answering:

  1. Which English terms and phrases recur in authenticated CI-DIGITAL sources, by provenance class and time?
  2. Which reviewed concepts do those terms designate in VLSI/IC design, verification, or test?
  3. Which artifact, activity, concern, method, lifecycle, and evidence facets apply?
  4. Which relations are observations, which are reviewed semantic assertions, and which are external mappings?
  5. What exact evidence and lineage, counterevidence, review, verification, validation, approval decision, and publication event supports each published item?
  6. What changed between releases, what depended on the change, and what was re-verified and/or re-validated?

If the implementation answers these questions without provenance leakage or semantic inflation, it has achieved a defensible concept index and typed concept graph. Formal ontology work can then be justified by concrete competency questions rather than by the appeal of a more formal label.

19. Recommended engineering decision

Adopt this methodology as the baseline for a future pilot, with four binding controls:

  1. Evidence identity before semantics: no published item without exact source/derivation traceability.
  2. VLSI domain independence: no cross-domain meaning without an explicit interface scheme and scoped mapping.
  3. Typed epistemic states: observation, candidate, reviewed assertion, verification, validation, approval, and publication are separate records.
  4. Lifecycle assurance: every material change triggers impact analysis, re-verification and/or re-validation according to the affected property, an independent release decision, and a separately recorded publication outcome.

The immediate next deliverable should be Stage 0: a compact governance specification and schema prototype derived from Sections 5, 6, 10, 16, and 17. Corpus ingestion should wait until those controls can be tested against the negative fixture.
