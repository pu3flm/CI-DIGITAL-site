The Scholastic Decree

Author

Fernando Lessa Magalhães

Updated

September 14, 2026

Tackling the space.

A draft declaration for CI-DIGITAL, developed from the author’s statements and source material. Its wording remains open to revision.

STICK TO THE PRINCIPLES, TO THE CORE OF THE FUNDAMENTALS.

Try at least once. Try to improve the second attempt. If it fails, at least we tried.

I. Begin with what must be understood

Ask what exists in the domain, what distinctions matter, how its concepts relate, and what human intent the work serves. Ask what is behind the assembly: its relations, constraints, dependencies, and conditions of operation. Follow the architecture on which the question relies. Let a difficulty in the answer send the inquiry upstream again.

II. Follow the behavior

Examine what an arrangement can do through its roles, couplings, conditions, and transitions. Similar parts can participate in different relations: lending includes an expected return that dispatch alone does not. Try changing a connection or a sequence. The behavior may expose a distinction that the present description cannot express. A domain can become visible through the way something changes.

III. Attend to meaning at the interface

A conceptual contract, a programming construct, and a bundle of signals can share the word “interface” while carrying different responsibilities. Ask what the term means here and what each representation can express. Domain architecture clarifies concepts and relations; executable architecture embodies part of that understanding. An encounter can give us reason to revise either description. Keep the intended relationship intelligible through that change.

IV. Enter Prototime

Try at least once. Assemble, couple, interrupt, vary a relation: put the idea into an attempt and see what happens. The attempt may change the arrangement, expose a relation, or leave the question unresolved.

Try to improve the second attempt. Change a connection, a stimulus, an interpretation, or the way you observe. Failure does not owe us a lesson. If it fails, at least we tried.

V. Keep the unplaced fact available

Try the analogy and let the attempt disclose which relations travel between domains and which differences matter. A different transition may change the meaning of a familiar arrangement.

Keep available what the present model cannot place. Failure to fit does not, by itself, make a fact false. It can trouble a comparison, redirect a question, or become useful in another attempt. There need not already be a category waiting for it.

VI. Ask what correctness means here

Ask what human purpose makes the expected behavior relevant, how the model expresses it, and what an observation establishes. A scoreboard’s evaluative role and its class structure answer different questions.

Built-in self-test belongs in this inquiry. Which fault conditions is it intended to detect, under which assumptions, and what escapes its observations? Passing that check does not establish the correctness of the entire design. When improving the next attempt, make visible what changed in the artifact, the observation, or the meaning of “pass.”

VII. Attend to the interval

The world can change between observing and acting; an action can change it again. Follow what changes and what persists. An observation belongs to a target, an occasion, and conditions.

Let old observations meet new questions. Keep their original question in view; explore which relations they still illuminate and where changed conditions leave them silent. A new use of a record does not rewrite what happened.

VIII. Know what the model lets us ask

A model of emergency sensing, reporting, and command can leave actual firefighting outside its account. Follow the command toward the activity it is meant to influence. Examine the response, delay, and feedback the model can express, and the questions that require looking beyond it.

Formalization gives us ways to investigate selected properties of a representation. Finding no inconsistency does not settle whether that representation captures what matters in the domain. A further attempt may extend the model or narrow the claim.

IX. Keep the human question present

A reply offers something the person can try against their question, reshape, or reject. An exchange may reveal a relation neither participant had articulated. Understanding and intention can develop through the encounter. Leave room for the person to say what they mean.

A contribution can extend the inquiry while respecting their authorship. An unsuccessful exchange may remain unsuccessful. Agreement, a lesson, or a new system is not owed as justification for having tried.

X. Carry the question into the next encounter

Keep the established concepts recognizable when using another vocabulary or tool. A new name needs an intelligible relationship to the meaning it carries. Bring philosophers, experiments, and other domains into the inquiry as sources of distinctions and further questions.

Follow what a new encounter still depends on from the first: an intended relation, a working connection, a shared word, or an unanswered question. Try again. What continues, and what has become another inquiry? The originating question can remain visible even when its terms change.

Tackling the space.

Source basis and relation to the specification

The opening principles come from the author’s statements in the current conversation. The second epigraph regularizes the spelling of: “LOOK, TRY AT LEAST ONCE, TRY TO IMPREOVE SECOND, IF IT FAILS, AT LEAST WE TRIED.” The articles are an assistant-authored draft developed through specialist exchanges, rather than a transcript of the author’s words.

  • Author’s Manifest, especially its distinction between conceptual and executable architecture, shifts in the meaning of “interface,” and the scoreboard example.
  • Opening project statement, retained in the private source collection, lines 1–13: methodology, human understanding, semantics, and intent are upstream; the master’s project supplies a proposed engineering payload. The later assistant reply in that file is not a source of adopted decisions.
  • Domain Theory: Foundations, retained in the private source collection, printed pages 85–129, corresponding to PDF pages 1–45: analogy, domain relations, transitions, sensing, explanatory dialogue, model boundaries, and the limits of its reported validation and formalization. The chapter’s principles are source proposals; they have not all been adopted into this decree.
  • Temporal-coherence evidence paper: observations, target identity, time, conditions, and the distinction between recorded evidence and current claims. Its historical experiments have not been rerun here.
  • Foundation and purpose and Cadence documentation and payload note: working drafts connecting the inquiry to the proposed engineering cases, including BIST.
  • Philosophical affinities: the accompanying map of thinkers and approaches to knowing, with sources and proposed connections.

The present repository contribution remains a specification, plan, and research presentation. The proposed beamformer and BIST work give the inquiry concrete cases; implementation and measured results require their own evidence as the work develops.
