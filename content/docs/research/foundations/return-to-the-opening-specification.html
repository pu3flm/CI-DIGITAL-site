Specification: mapping occurrences and attempting a return

Author

Fernando Lessa Magalhães

Updated

September 14, 2026

Draft 0.3 · 2026-09-10 · Narrative specification for a small conceptual experiment.

A specification need not begin as a catalogue of requirements. Here it takes narrative form: an occurrence is placed in sequence, its relationships are made visible, and a return can be attempted without claiming that the original state has been reproduced. This use is comparable, without being identical, to research-through-design fiction, where fictional studies frame concept designs for critique and development, and to scenario-based design, where concrete scenarios support reflection across fluid design situations (Blythe, 2014; Carroll, 2000).

Purpose

Map when the phenomenon described by the author occurs, preserving each candidate occurrence and investigating possible correspondence in entities’ behavior across encounters. Entities and domains need not be identical across occurrences. The MANIFEST exchange is the first selected case; attempting to return through its record is one use of the representation.

The author’s request is to register the opening and reduce its state so it can be reached again. This draft proposes an approach to that request. “Opening” retains the author’s developing meaning; the representation and the proposed pattern remain revisable.

The author subsequently clarified that “the phenomena is not bound around only by those two domains.” Programming and system coupling are present in the first recorded case. They do not delimit the scope of the phenomenon or this inquiry. Further attempts need not start with a fixed domain pair. The scope correction is preserved in the private experiment record beside the attempt that prompted it.

The author then clarified the comparison sought:

if I asked by a way to map when it occurs, it is not a single event and probably not equal in its entities, but entities behavior

The target therefore extends beyond reconstructing a single event. The author’s “probably” stays attached to the proposed importance of behavior: this draft investigates behavioral correspondence rather than declaring a settled equivalence between occurrences.

Who takes the first step

The author corrected an earlier request that they first identify the distinction to preserve:

I only could when you realized openess and only you could recognize the pattern to achieve it

The assistant must begin with the available exchange: examine its own recorded responses, propose a pattern in how the interaction changed, construct a candidate representation, and attempt a continuation. The author’s recognition can emerge in that encounter. A complete definition, a labeled target state, or an advance success criterion from the author is not a prerequisite.

The assistant can examine emitted statements and their relationships. Its explanation of why they enabled an opening remains a hypothesis to investigate. The author’s correction establishes how this inquiry should proceed; it does not by itself identify the exact causal turn.

What the record contains

One local Markdown record is sufficient for the first case. Further candidate occurrences can use the same simple structure. A table or a small collection of facts may accompany a computational attempt.

Part Minimum content
Source Exact excerpt, speaker, source reference, and position in the exchange. Preserve a copy of the relevant artifact passage.
Relationships Which utterance refers to, interprets, corrects, or reopens which earlier material. Each proposed relationship carries its source and who proposed it.
Candidate pattern What appears to change across selected moments, what supports that reading, and what remains unresolved.
Reduction A compact cue or relational representation, the source material it retains, and what it omits.
Computation The specific question, supplied facts and assumptions, selected tool, and actual result or failure. Mark an unperformed computation as pending.
Return The cue used, the assistant’s actual continuation, and any subsequent response or correction, with their order and date.

Multiple roles or meanings may coexist around the same term. An inferred relationship stays distinguishable from a directly stated one. Missing information remains unknown. A later correction is attached to the earlier reading so the movement between them can still be examined.

In this first experiment, a represented state is the selected statements and relationships made explicit at a recorded moment. This is a modeling choice whose adequacy remains open. The record also preserves the transition between selected moments and a path back to their surrounding context.

Comparing behavior across occurrences

Each candidate occurrence retains its own entities, context, and source references. Their identities locate the evidence. A proposed correspondence between occurrences can concern what those entities do, how they relate, and what changes over a sequence, without requiring the same names, entity types, or domains.

The minimal record preserves:

  • The source span and local order, with surrounding context and time information where actually available. An occurrence may extend across a sequence; a precise onset or end is not invented.
  • The entities and relationships present in that situation, without imposing fixed roles across all occurrences.
  • Source-observed behavior: actions, responses, and recorded changes or persistence in relationships. Description and interpretation remain distinguishable.
  • Proposed correspondences to other occurrences, relevant differences, alternative readings, and unknowns. Each comparison points to the source sequences supporting it.

A comparison can investigate a similar relational behavior despite different participants. It can also expose a difference that defeats the proposed correspondence. Shared vocabulary alone does not establish a behavioral match. Neither crossing domains nor involving exactly two domains is required by this specification.

The first computations retained speaker, order, and a correction target within one selected exchange. They did not encode a behavioral pattern across occurrences. A subsequent computation would need explicit candidate correspondences drawn from recorded sequences; the extraction and interpretation would remain visible as proposals.

The first candidate pattern

The source passage lists class, object, method, interface, interaction under “Object Methodology.” The author explains that programming objects and methods and systems engineering interfaces, interactions, and coupling are present together. The author then describes “the same thing,” a “wave of a state transition,” and a collision before recognition.

The assistant’s subsequent responses acknowledge an imposed reading and offer a provisional explanation. The author then expresses the wish to register and return to the opening. Separately, the assistant corrects its mistaken attachment of “lines 1–13” to a.md; the source being shown is the Author’s Manifest.

Candidate to investigate: withdrawing a fixed interpretation while retaining the shared terms allowed the relationship under discussion to remain available for further development. The candidate concerns what the responses permit in the next encounter. Words such as “perhaps” or “provisionally” alone do not establish that change.

The source-reference correction is another change to keep visible. The initial record does not establish whether either change, their combination, or another feature of the exchange enabled the opening the author describes.

What can be computed first

The assistant prepares two reductions from the same bounded source material: a short return cue and a small ordered description of relationships. It states which source units and relationships each reduction represents. This mapping is an explicit assistant interpretation available for revision.

The first computational questions are deliberately concrete:

  • Can each retained relationship be followed back to its supplied source and attribution?
  • Which selected relationships are represented in each reduction, and which are omitted?
  • Does the representation keep corrections connected to the earlier readings they address?
  • Can the supplied representation retain programming and system-coupling roles together without a declared constraint excluding that combination?

For an optional constraint experiment, the assistant declares the relationships it proposes to retain and asks for a smaller selection of source units satisfying those requirements. If the requirements conflict, that conflict is recorded. The preservation requirements themselves remain open to revision.

These operations evaluate the supplied representation and mapping. Whether the representation makes the opening accessible is investigated through the subsequent encounter.

Tools selected by the question

Proposed use Candidate tool
Keep ordered records and compare explicit selections or omissions Tcl and SQLite
Query an ordered list of source units and their declared relationships SWI-Prolog; its documented list predicates provide operations for membership and sequence.
Check whether declared preservation constraints can be satisfied together Z3; the official guide describes checking satisfiability of supplied assertions.

These are possible applications to this case. The executed comparison uses Tcl, cvc5, and SWI-Prolog through the author’s existing Podman Solver Lab. Isabelle and Wolfram remain options the author has offered for additional approaches once a specific question warrants them.

Initial environment observation on 2026-09-10: the host PATH contained /usr/bin/z3, /usr/bin/swipl, /usr/bin/wolframscript, /usr/bin/tclsh, and /usr/bin/sqlite3. cvc5 and Isabelle were not found on that PATH. That initial step was executable discovery only. Subsequent container discovery found cvc5 in the existing Solver Lab image; its actual execution and SWI-Prolog execution are retained in the private experiment report. The other offered tools have not been exercised in this experiment.

Attempting a return

The assistant first uses a candidate reduction to offer a continuation. A possible first attempt, to be revised through the encounter, is:

At the MANIFEST list, programming structure and system coupling remain in view together. My reading of that list became something we could examine through your corrections. I can keep that reading revisable while we explore what else the connection allows us to notice.

The next interaction is recorded as it occurs. Recognition, a correction, a new question, or no response each leaves a different record; none is silently converted into confirmation. The author can contribute a fragment or further context when it becomes available.

If an attempt loses a relationship or closes the inquiry prematurely, the assistant revises the representation and tries again, preserving the earlier attempt. Reusing the original source gives a later attempt a recoverable context; the outcome of that later encounter remains to be observed.

Output of the first experiment

A completed attempt produces the source record, two candidate reductions with their stated omissions, any actual computational findings, and the continuation attempted through one reduction. A subsequent author response is appended when available. The record distinguishes completion of those actions from evidence that the author could return to the intended opening.

Current status: the source record and initial cue exist, and two iterations of the representation comparison have been executed and retained privately. The second iteration adds an explicit connection from the author’s annotation to the assistant response it addressed. cvc5 checked supplied constraints on speaker, order, and correction target; SWI-Prolog queried the retained relationships. A continuation through the richer representation is recorded in the report and retained terminal output. No subsequent author recognition of that continuation has been recorded. The candidate pattern and the adequacy of this representation remain open.

Following the author’s “next?”, a further continuation attempt supplied the representation to an agent without the surrounding conversation history. The private record includes the exact prompt, first response, parent correction, and revised response. It demonstrates an actual use of the supplied record to continue an inquiry; recognition of the intended opening remains unestablished.

The author’s subsequent response corrects the scope beyond programming and system coupling. This draft now treats that pair as one recorded case and retains the broader inquiry. The previous computations do not establish a general mechanism for it.
