Approach: investigating the assembly

Author

Fernando Lessa Magalhães

Updated

September 14, 2026

AI-Pipeline Tools for VLSI Workflows is Group 6’s individual project, developed with AI assistance. This page defines its working approach for the specification and presentation. The central interest is how engineering intent becomes a useful arrangement of concepts, tools, observations, and explanations across a VLSI workflow.

“What is the assembly of it?” asks what makes that arrangement possible. A tool’s visible command is an entrance to its dependencies: the information it receives, the mechanism that processes it, the environment in which it operates, and the assumptions that make its output meaningful. Following those dependencies upstream helps locate the actual problem. In a UART investigation, for example, interpreting a reported mismatch involves the expected transaction, the observed signals, the sampling event, and the meaning of their timestamps.

The Author’s Manifest develops a central distinction within this inquiry: domain meaning and executable organization answer different engineering questions. Understanding a scoreboard involves its relationship to expected and observed behavior; organizing its software involves responsibilities, methods, and communication. Both descriptions matter. The relational domain ontology proposal extends this concern to the relationships connecting purpose, constraints, decisions, and artifacts as work passes through successive representations. Behavior is especially useful here: similar names can conceal different roles, while different entities may exhibit relationships worth comparing.

Prototime names the practice of trying conceptual prototypes and proofs of concept. A small trial gives a question something concrete to encounter: a representation, an observation, an unexpected result, or a limitation. Its value includes discovering that an initial description leaves something out. The next formulation can then become more precise. This gives conceptual inquiry a practical rhythm while allowing independent experiments to retain their own questions.

Native computational tools support that rhythm through mechanisms suited to the task. C++ offers direct structured processing; Tcl expresses coordination; SQLite organizes relations and queries; logic languages express logical conditions. Examining these foundations helps explain why a particular representation or tool belongs in an arrangement. Tool choice follows the operation and the domain relationships it needs to preserve.

AI participation spans implementation, documentation, and learning. The C++ waveform helper joins a concrete programming task with explanatory comments, readable output, and a classroom story. The concept-index case investigates how vocabulary and relationships can become searchable records and graph views. Temporal Coherence examines what may change between an observation and a later action. The Cadence and BIST work brings simulation, synthesis, internal test mechanisms, and engineering documentation into the specification.

These cases give the VLSI main line several concrete contributions while preserving their independent purposes. The present work elaborates their questions, dependencies, proposed specifications, and explanatory examples into a coherent academic presentation.

Objectives

The work sets out to:

  • establish the purpose, fundamental concepts, boundaries, and architectural questions the rest depends on;
  • explain how an AI-assisted C++ process connects a UART failure to a usable technical account;
  • specify how concepts and relationships can be organized for navigation within engineering work;
  • describe a Cadence workflow with explicit simulation, synthesis, and BIST responsibilities;
  • define the digital control and test questions in the RF application context, including the beamforming direction;
  • explain how changing state and timing affect the relation between observation, reasoning, and action;
  • consolidate the cases into a presentation that communicates the approach.

These are O1–O7 in the work plan, where each is paired with an activity, a deliverable, and the criteria that complete it.

Boundaries

The subject is architectural investigation, specification, elaboration of existing work, and presentation. The existing tools and experiments supply the material the cases examine.

Outside it: FPGA implementation, fabrication, new laboratory campaigns, and deployment. Where a case requires one of these, it is planned separately with its own dependencies and estimate rather than absorbed into the present scope.

Two distinctions hold throughout the text. Appearing together here does not make the contributions part of one implemented pipeline; each retains the purpose it was built for. And existing results, their interpretation, and proposed continuation stay separable, so that a reader can tell which is which.
