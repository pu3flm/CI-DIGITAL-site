Concept Index: Giving Every Connection a Source

Author

Fernando Lessa Magalhães

Updated

September 14, 2026

Finding the engineering behind the words

An engineering project leaves a trail of specifications, source files, verification plans, results and conversations. An index can help someone find a term across that material. A graph can make connections visible. The challenge is deciding what those connections mean.

The CI-DIGITAL concept-index investigation asks how to make recurring language useful for VLSI design, verification and test while retaining its source and context. One practical objective is to follow a concept such as functional coverage back to the definitions, project artifacts and evidence that explain its use.

This gives the question “What is the assembly of it?” a concrete application: inspect what supports a connection and what happened between the original material and the displayed result.

What the earlier pilot exposed

The methodology records an earlier “Create Concept Index” pilot built from 18 English interaction records. At its chosen threshold, the sample yielded no repeated contiguous phrases of two to four words. The useful finding was the sample’s limit: a claim about recurring phrases needed more evidence.

Other findings concerned the graph itself. Declared concept assignments appeared alongside measured co-occurrences. Changing a scoring formula raised scores without adding source material. Projecting connections to a domain level reused the same evidence, while concepts without connections disappeared from an edge-based display.

Together, these findings exposed a presentation problem with engineering consequences: a graph can appear richer or more certain without acquiring additional support. The methodology preserves these findings as reported project requirements; its cited original historical exports were unavailable in the repository.

From visible patterns to explainable meaning

The proposed method keeps three kinds of information distinct:

  • Source material: preserve the source, author or tool, version and location.
  • Measured patterns: record occurrences and co-occurrences under a stated procedure.
  • Engineering meaning: create a separate interpretation with a definition, scope, supporting material and review.

For example, finding “coverage” beside “closure” produces an observation. A claim about their engineering relationship needs its own explanation and evidence. A copied passage or a graph projection still leads back to the same underlying source.

The index also allows several perspectives on one concept: the artifact it concerns, the activity it supports, its lifecycle role and its evidence role. A coverage model can belong to several useful views without being forced into one hierarchy. External vocabularies retain their definitions, with explicit mappings where a correspondence is useful.

This case uses an English-language inclusion rule and a VLSI-specific domain boundary. Those choices define the proposed index’s corpus; the wider master’s repository continues to hold independent research and writing. The methodology reserves the word ontology for a later commitment to explicit formal meanings and inference rules.

Making selected ideas concrete

The existing local Python/SQLite mock-up represents these distinctions through separate records for sources, observations, candidates and reviewed claims. Its views keep an observation graph distinct from a reviewed semantic graph, retain concepts without edges, and preserve links to the material behind a displayed connection. Versioned records describe changes without overwriting earlier history.

The application described in its README uses local, deterministic processing without an LLM or network service. It remains a mock-up; the README records static authoring checks and leaves runtime acceptance unverified.

Within AI-Pipeline Tools for VLSI Workflows, this contribution addresses the information that a pipeline depends on: terms, meanings, relationships and their origins. Its concrete result is a methodology and a local representation of selected ideas that make the path from source material to a proposed engineering connection inspectable.
