CI-DIGITAL Common Ontology

Author

Fernando Lessa Magalhães

Updated

September 14, 2026

Status and scope

This document is the project-level, controlled-language specification of the CI-DIGITAL common ontology. It records the vocabulary, relations, and constraints to which descriptions of the project currently commit.

Its entity of interest is the CI-DIGITAL project. It constrains project descriptions; it does not claim that different participants have identical internal representations.

This document is not yet a machine-formal OWL ontology or a SHACL shapes graph. Consequently, the present conformance review is documentary and finite rather than a claim of logical or universal conformance.

Canonical foundation

The terminology in this section comes from established ontology and architecture-description work rather than from project-specific paraphrase.

  • Common ontology: a shared representational vocabulary, including relations and constraints, used for consistent exchange among participating agents. Gruber distinguishes consistency with a common ontology from completeness or identity of internal knowledge.
  • Ontological commitment: the intended interpretation of a vocabulary to which descriptions or participating agents commit. Guarino treats an ontology as a logical theory that constrains admissible models toward intended models.
  • Interpretation and model: under OWL 2 model-theoretic semantics, an interpretation that satisfies the ontology’s axioms is a model of that ontology.
  • Architecture viewpoint: ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010 uses this term for conventions governing the construction, interpretation, and use of architecture views. A viewpoint is distinct from an ontology.
  • Conformance: SHACL uses a shapes graph to validate a data graph and report whether it conforms. This is distinct from logical satisfaction under OWL semantics.

Let O denote an ontology and I an interpretation. The formal relation used by OWL is:

\[ I \models O \]

when I satisfies every applicable axiom in O. The set of admissible interpretations is therefore:

\[ \operatorname{Mod}(O)=\{I\mid I\models O\}. \]

The ontology constrains this set. It does not generally reduce it to one interpretation.

Current ontological commitment

The following statements are the current controlled-language commitments of the project reference. They are not presented as formal OWL axioms:

  1. CI-DIGITAL is the entity of interest of this project ontology.
  2. Content is an established project domain for authored, conceptual, reader-facing, and structured-knowledge material. Storage under content/ can represent this domain but does not establish membership by location alone.
  3. Session has a Domain role relative to Content and a Context role relative to the domains admitted inside Session. These are qualified, Session-specific relations, not universal equivalence between Context and Domain.
  4. Relative to Session, Transcript is an artifact that provides evidence for derived concept lineages. The artifact is not identical to an interpretation derived from it.
  5. Worktree is the accepted current project term for an umbrella of repository-shaped work units. It is explicitly not the Git-worktree concept, and its permanent name remains unresolved.
  6. A filesystem path or file is a representation or storage location. Its name, nesting, or proximity does not by itself establish semantic identity, domain membership, subclassing, or an interface.
  7. A project relation is asserted only when its source and adoption status support it. Otherwise the relation remains unresolved.
  8. Source statements, assistant interpretations, adopted terms, tool observations, and derived conclusions remain distinguishable through conceptual provenance.

Interpretation constraints

A project description is consistent with this common ontology only when it:

  1. identifies the entity being described;
  2. states the relevant relation and its scope;
  3. distinguishes a conceptual entity from its representation and storage location;
  4. preserves the status and attribution of the relation;
  5. does not infer semantics from a filename or directory hierarchy alone; and
  6. leaves unsupported relations unresolved rather than completing them by symmetry or analogy.

These constraints replace the earlier assistant claim that CI-DIGITAL, Content, Session, Worktree, Hardware, and Software were all simultaneously Contexts and Domains. That generalization is withdrawn. The present sources establish the qualified dual role for Session; the corresponding roles of the other entities require separate grounding.

Bounded production review

The current review sample is exactly three repository work products. It is evidence about these three products only.

The engineer established the evaluation relation as a comparison between what is expected and what is obtained, followed by the state that should result. In verification terminology:

\[ \Delta = \operatorname{compare}(E,A) \]

\[ R = \operatorname{resolve}(A,\Delta) \]

where E is the expected result, A is the actual observed result, \(\Delta\) is the discrepancy, and R is the resulting state. A correct output is not the observation alone or an unexplained pass label. It is the traceable resulting state whose relevant discrepancies with the expectation were resolved or explicitly retained.

Conceptual provenance and corrections

  1. The engineer identified the need for a common reference through which multiple participants could interpret the project structure consistently.
  2. The assistant initially restated exploratory project wording as though it were canonical terminology and generalized Context/Domain duality across six named entities.
  3. The engineer corrected both moves: use terminology grounded in prior research, and do not make a universal claim when a finite production review is the available evidence.
  4. Primary sources established the applicable distinctions: common ontology, ontological commitment, interpretation, model, architecture viewpoint, and conformance.
  5. The assistant consolidated those distinctions into this project-specific controlled-language ontology and withdrew the unsupported universal generalization.
  6. The engineer set the validation boundary to a finite number of production work outputs. The initial number is three, followed by the review recorded above.
  7. The engineer corrected the review criterion: correctness is established through the trace from expected result, through actual observation and discrepancy, to the resulting state. The agent replaced unexplained pass labels with that trace.

The project-specific commitments and their organization are an agent-authored consolidation requested by the engineer. The research terminology retains its external attribution. The finite review does not convert unresolved project relations into adopted commitments.

Formalization boundary

A later machine-readable stage may serialize the adopted vocabulary and axioms as an OWL 2 ontology and express closed data-quality constraints in a separate SHACL shapes graph. That stage has not been performed. It requires stable identifiers and explicit decisions about which project terms denote classes, individuals, or qualified relations.
